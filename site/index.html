How a tip becomes a moment

Viewers already pay to be noticed. This gives them something to buy that happens on your camera, where the whole room sees it. Below is the hijack playing above, seen from the viewer's phone, your stream, and your dashboard.

    A viewer pays for a look

    Your portal page lists the looks you allow. The viewer picks one, gets a short code, and tips with the code in the message. The tip goes to you through Streamlabs, as it does today.

MISCHIEF, WITH BOUNDARIES

Chat gets the spotlight.
You keep the controls.

  • You build the menu. Turn looks on or off, set the minimum tip and the longest a hijack can run.
  • Custom prompts are off until you say. When you allow them, a blocklist and a moderation pass run before anything reaches your camera.
  • One key ends it. ⌘⇧H from anywhere cuts back to your camera and pauses the queue.
  • Every hijack ends on time. Three separate timers cap the run: one in the app, one watchdog on your machine, and one on the model provider's servers. If any of them fire, your camera comes back.

One source in OBS. Nothing else changes.

The app connects to OBS Studio over its WebSocket server and adds a single Browser Source named AI Hijack to your scene. It stays hidden until a hijack is live, then hides again. Your scenes, filters, alerts and camera stay exactly as you had them.

It reads your camera from the OBS Virtual Camera, so whatever you already do to your picture in OBS carries over into the hijack.

Questions

What do I need?
OBS Studio with its WebSocket server on, a Streamlabs account for tips, and the Reality Hijack desktop app. It builds for macOS today.
Is this a Twitch extension?
No. Tips come in through Streamlabs, so it works on any platform where you take tips that way. No Bits, no extension review.
Where does the money go?
To you, through Streamlabs, exactly as your tips do now. The app only listens for the tip event; it never touches the payment.
How long does a hijack last?
As many seconds as the tip, up to the maximum you set. A short wipe covers the cut in and the cut out.
Is there a delay?
The restyled picture runs a little behind your camera, as any live AI video does. The wipe hides the jump at both ends, and the countdown is on screen so viewers know what is happening.
What can a viewer make it do?
Only a look you have turned on. Custom prompts are off unless you allow them, and when you do, they are checked against your guardrails before they run.
Does it cost me anything?
Not during alpha. Pricing after alpha is not decided yet.
